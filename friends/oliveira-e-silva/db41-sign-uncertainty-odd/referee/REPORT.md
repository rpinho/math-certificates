For the lower, I independently rederived these steps for arbitrary odd ell>=3. Suppose the original constraints are feasible at K=ell-1. On even vectors D^2=I, so u=(f-Df)/2 satisfies Du=-u, u(0)>=0, u(n)>=0 for |n|>=ell-1, and u(q)>=1. Thus projection does not change either endpoint requirement or the common cutoff. Any smaller cutoff also satisfies the constraints at ell-1 by inclusion of inequalities.

Let C_r indicate n=r mod ell, delta=2pi/N, c(n)=cos(delta*n), s(n)=sin(delta*n), P=C_1+C_-1, R=C_1-C_-1, and t=tan(delta/2). For the submitted G=2C_0+2cC_0+P-cP-tsR, the finite geometric sum gives D(C_r)(k)=exp(-i*delta*k*r)C_0(k). Modulation by exp(+-i*delta*n) shifts the transform by +-1. Applying these rules to the five terms reduces DG=G to cos(delta)+t*sin(delta)=1 and sin(delta)=t*(1+cos(delta)); both are exact half-angle identities.

I checked the crucial positivity by a separate factorization. On C_0, G(n)=4cos^2(pi*n/N)>0 since N is odd. On C_1, for 0<=n<N,

    G(n)=2 sin(pi*n/N) sin(pi*(n-1)/N) / cos(pi/N).

Here n=1 is its only zero; every other n in C_1 is >=2, making both sine factors positive. Reflect for C_-1. Every potentially negative inner coordinate is either outside these classes or is +-1, so it has G=0. The origin is nonnegative. Consequently <u,G>=0 forces u to vanish on C_0 and on C_+-1 except possibly +-1. At k=j*ell, Du(k)=0 says that the length-ell DFT of the coset sums is zero. Invertibility makes every coset sum zero. Since only u(1) or u(-1) remains in those two classes, they vanish too. No pointwise conclusion was drawn from coset sums alone.

For n=r+m*ell, 1<=r<=ell-1 and 0<=m<=ell-1, the submitted second separator has the independent factorization

    H(n)=2 sin(pi*m/ell) sin(pi*(m+1)/ell),

and H=0 on C_0. Thus H is nonnegative, and vanishes exactly at m=0,ell-1 in every nonzero class. These are all the nonzero coordinates |n|<=ell-1. As a function of m it has only frequencies 0,+1,-1, so direct geometric summation puts DH inside C_0 union C_1 union C_-1. Since u vanishes there, unitarity gives <u,H>=<Du,DH>=0. All terms of <u,H> are nonnegative. Writing ell=2h+1 gives q=h*ell+h and H(q)=1+cos(pi/ell)>0, contradicting u(q)>=1. This proves the universal lower. No limiting, derivative, monotonicity-in-q or statistical assumption enters.

The optional stronger zero-vector assertion also follows: H eliminates all coordinates |n|>=ell, and G eliminates +- (ell-1). A vector and its transform then both have support within [-(ell-2),ell-2]. Its polynomial of degree <=2ell-4 would have N-(2ell-3) distinct roots; the excess over the degree is (ell-2)^2+3>0. The endpoint contradiction already suffices.

For the upper I rebuilt g(n)=sin(2pi*|n|/ell) on |n|<=ell and zero elsewhere, then formed g-Dg with the original operator. The paper's closed formula gives a nonnegative tail from ell onward (removable value zero at ell), a negative value at ell-1, zero at the origin and positive value at q. Positive rescaling enforces both normalized endpoints; the unscaled seed need not itself have endpoint >=1. This is the published baseline, not a numerical improvement.
