# Independent analytic audit

Reviewed the statement in CLAIM.md and the claimant's PROOF.md against the primary paper, not against the claimant's symbolic checks. No claimant program was read or imported. The submitted JSON paths were used as data only.

The objective is exactly the positive-weight, scalar-parameter identity-design objective in Eq.(2) of [Bento–Furmaniak–Ray](https://arxiv.org/html/1801.04987v3). T counts maximal affine pieces of x(lambda), including the final constant piece. It is not fixed-parameter runtime, number of different fusion partitions, an empirical prediction score, or the number of events counted with multiplicity. The paper gives the unrestricted quadratic order; this claim restricts the weights.

The following is my own check of the essential argument. It also gives a way to handle simultaneous events directly, without needing to rely on the claimant's generic perturbation.

Let c_i=sum_{j<=i} y_j, c_0=0, and z_i=sum_{j<=i} x_j. The chain dual/KKT representation minimizes one half the squared slopes of z subject to z_0=0, z_n=c_n, and |z_i-c_i|<=lambda*a_i. Summation/differencing is an invertible linear map with the endpoint fixed, so it preserves the number of affine pieces. This is the paper's Theorem 1 after a constant shift of coordinates. If l,r are adjacent contacts with signs s_l,s_r, z_i between them is their linear interpolation. Endpoints have slope zero because a_0=a_n=0.

**The definition of the good set is correct.** For discrete weights, the least concave majorant H(i) is the greatest height at i in the convex hull of the data points (j,a_j). A maximization over probability mixtures with mean index i has an optimizer with at most two support points. Therefore a_i=H(i) exactly when a_i dominates every chord from l<i<r. This includes all collinear contacts, not just strict hull vertices. The endpoint indices are good.

**Good contacts cannot return.** On an open affine interval where i is free, let l,r be its adjacent contacts. The magnitude of z_i's lambda derivative is at most the interpolation of a_l and a_r. For good i that is at most a_i. Both nonnegative gaps from z_i to its two box boundaries consequently have nonnegative derivatives while i is free. The finitely many contact changes preserve continuity. Once either gap is strictly positive on a free interval, neither can reach zero later. Thus each internal good coordinate has at most one departure. Its sign while attached cannot change for lambda>0, since its upper and lower boundaries are distinct. A touch at an isolated later parameter cannot evade this gap argument.

**The exponential bound counts affine pieces correctly.** There are at most g-2 departures of good coordinates, hence at most g-1 open phases with their statuses fixed. For fixed statuses of the k other coordinates there is a unique affine interpolant. Free box inequalities and contact gradient signs are affine inequalities in lambda. The exact pattern's validity set, including strict inequalities for coordinates declared free, is an interval. It cannot recur in separated parts of a phase. At most 3^k patterns occur per phase. Continuity prevents isolated ties from adding pieces. Therefore T<=3^k(g-1), including tied inputs and collinear weights.

**The pair count is sound.** A pair of good coordinates that are adjacent in the full contact set must also be adjacent in the active good set. An ordered set of g good indices that only loses internal elements has at most g-1 initial adjacency pairs and g-2 subsequently created pairs. The other possible pairs number at most g*k + k(k-1)/2. Thus the number of endpoint pairs ever usable for a free gap is bounded by

    P = 2g-3 + gk + k(k-1)/2.

**At most eight distinct critical values per pair, including simultaneous changes.** At any actual change of affine formula, the contact patterns on its two open sides must differ: if they did not, the same interpolation would determine both formulas. Choose a changing coordinate j and the side where j is strictly free; let l,r be its adjacent contacts on that side. Their signs are fixed on that open interval. The corresponding chord is strictly inside every intervening box there. The set of parameters for which the chord is feasible in all those boxes is an intersection of scalar halfspaces, hence an interval. At the event, j reaches its boundary by continuity. This particular chord-to-boundary affine difference is not an identity, because it was strictly positive on the free side. Therefore the event is a finite endpoint of that feasibility interval. There are at most two endpoints for each of four sign pairs. This charging works even if multiple contacts change at once: choose any changed j and its own free-side gap. It bounds distinct knots, which is what T requires. Coordinates that touch only at an isolated parameter cannot by themselves change the affine formula when the open-side patterns agree.

Combining these facts gives T<=1+8P, with no genericity requirement. This independently closes the two main risks flagged in the claim. The claimant's alternative perturbation argument is also consistent: adding epsilon*i*(n-i) makes all original good chord inequalities strict, the extra slack is epsilon*(r-l)*(i-l)*(r-i), and sufficiently small generic perturbations preserve that good set. Uniform limits on compact parameter intervals cannot increase a fixed bound on the number of affine pieces.

**The asymptotic statement follows with its stated quantifiers.** With g=n+1-k, P=(k+2)n-1-(k^2+3k)/2. Thus T=O(n(k+1)) for all real y, and bounded k gives linear order. There is no assertion of a sharp constant or a faster path algorithm. Decreasing at most k entries of a positive zero-padded concave sequence preserves the all-chord inequalities at every unchanged entry, so the stated sufficient family is valid. Raising a weight is not interchangeable with this operation.

**Controls and prior results.** The k=0 case is already implied by [Tibshirani–Taylor 2011, Section 6, Eq.(23)](https://www.stat.berkeley.edu/~ryantibs/papers/genlasso.pdf): with D=diag(a)*chain differences, diagonal dominance of D D^T reduces to 2a_i>=a_(i-1)+a_(i+1). This does not explain the nonconcave cases with an actual unfusion. Already the source paper's Fig.1 has k=1 and T=5, whereas the equal-weight control for that input has T=3. The proposed bound for that nonconcave example is 9, not an improvement on the observed value 5. Setting its low weight to zero changes the objective and fails the original KKT conditions. [Le–Nguyen–Nguyen 2026, Appendix G.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.02406v2#A7.SS2) also explicitly uses the general 3^(d-1) signed-box-state bound, in a multiple-hyperparameter learnability result. The proposed refinement relative to the 2018 paper is controlling the number of states/events using the envelope deficit parameter k. No novelty, priority, or author-interest judgment is made.

**Evidence limits.** The 570 exact finite cases are diagnostic corroboration; the all-n conclusion rests on the argument above. They are not a statistical holdout, and no holdout is needed to prove an assertion quantified over every y. The independent adversarial test inputs were generated after the claimant's files were fixed and were never used to retune the claimed theorem. The proof is a human-readable mathematical audit, not a proof-assistant certificate. Literature searches have the explicitly recorded indexing and GitHub rate-limit gaps.
