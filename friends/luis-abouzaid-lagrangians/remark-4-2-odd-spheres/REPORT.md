Independent referee of L063, 2026-11-02. The proposed odd-sphere result survives as a corollary of the integral split-generation statement actually printed in Abouzaid–Diogo Proposition 4.1. The caveat is foundational: I verified that statement against the journal, but did not reconstruct its integral Floer-theoretic proof. Its cited general criterion, Ritter–Smith, is formulated over a field. This is a limitation of this audit, not a counterexample or an assertion that Proposition 4.1 is false.

The journal version, Advances in Mathematics 427 (2023), 109114, p21, agrees with arXiv v2: Proposition 4.1 uses K_R, Section 3.4 says R is Z or C, and Remark 4.2 poses ordinary generation. The source supplies split-generation rather than ordinary generation for the monotone category; it supplies no cone count. Sections 3.1 and 3.3 use full real Novikov exponents and a direct limit of finite wrapping stages. The free integral fibre calculation in Lemma 4.3 and scalar extension give K_Z[u], with no completion in u. For odd n the generator has even parity. Section 3.5 explicitly restricts its formality machinery to the complex Novikov field; the submitted proof does not use that machinery. [Journal source](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1783421/FULLTEXT01.pdf), [arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.13478v2).

Authorship is confirmed by the journal title page and independently by [Diogo's research page](https://sites.google.com/view/ldiogomath/research) and the [IAS seminar record](https://www.ias.edu/video/puias/2016/1011-LuisDiogo). This is joint work with Mohammed Abouzaid.

I checked the universal proof by the following independent reasoning.

1. The substitution t^gamma -> T^(-gamma) takes the full ring in Farber–Schuetz Lemma 1 exactly to AD's K_Z; both permit arbitrary real exponents and locally finite support in the appropriate direction. The PID theorem therefore has the right ring, rather than a positive-valuation subring. A direct check of the PID argument also works: in a nonzero ideal choose an element with smallest positive absolute leading integer coefficient d. Aligning valuations and performing integer Euclidean division shows every nonzero ideal element has leading coefficient divisible by d. Successive cancellation divides any ideal element by the chosen one with integral coefficients. This process converges in the Novikov sense: its support lies in the original support plus the nonnegative monoid of positive exponent differences of the divisor. That set is finite below each bound. Thus no dense-exponent accumulation was silently allowed. [Farber–Schuetz, p5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0807.3240).

2. Set R=K_Z and S=R[u]. R is Noetherian and hereditary. The relative exact sequence with terms S tensor_R M, first map u tensor m minus 1 tensor um, bounds pd_S(M) by two. Injectivity follows from the largest polynomial degree and the cokernel from evaluation. Inducing an R-projective resolution bounds the projective dimensions of the induced terms by one. This argument uses no inversion of an integer.

3. The original Seshadri paper, p457, proves that finite projectives over a polynomial ring in one variable over a PID are free. I read pp456–458 as page scans, retained here as seshadri_456.png through seshadri_458.png. Thus the theorem has no missing field or finite-type hypothesis. For an even algebra the two parity pieces of a graded projective are projectives over S, so each is free. A degree-zero component of an ungraded section is a graded section; this requires no averaging or division by 2. [Original Seshadri scans](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC335443/), [Endo's explicit attribution, p339](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/15/3/15_3_339/_pdf).

4. Let h(X)=H*Hom(F,X). Noetherianity makes h(X) finite for every X in the thick closure of F: this property survives triangles and retracts. Choose homogeneous generators and represent them by closed maps from a finite sum P0 of shifted fibres. In K1 -> P0 -> X the surjection on h in both parities makes h(K1) the genuine first kernel. Repeat with P1 -> K1. Then h(K2) is a finite second syzygy, hence projective and graded free by steps 2–3.

5. Represent a homogeneous basis of h(K2) by a closed map P2 -> K2. Its cone Z has h(Z)=0 and remains in thick(F). The objects Y with Hom*(Y,Z)=0 form a thick subcategory containing F, hence contain Z. Its identity vanishes, so Z is zero. Working backwards through the two triangles gives X in the ordinary finite triangulated envelope of F. No asserted full faithfulness of a cohomology-module functor, minimal model, or formal idempotent splitting is needed.

6. Finite upper triangular twisted complexes only insert finitely many new cone arrows in each operation. Operations between the original objects are already defined; their existing bounding cochains are not changed. This justifies the algebraic unfiltered construction, including the paper's already admitted valuation-zero special sphere cochains. It does not justify filtered generation, infinite cones, a positive subring, arbitrary curved objects, or new local-system fibres.
