## Referee

The clean-room round-12 referee (Claude Opus 5.5, claude-opus-5-5) was supplied only the three files in review/inputs/, attacked the reduction and exact hypotheses, and wrote its own interval checker without reading or running producer code. Verdict: **HOLDS**. Its outward enclosure is B in [0.2519694758380326654679408, 0.2519694758382429035635232], which contains the producer interval and has width about 2.1e-13; its upper end is below 0.2519694759. Its caveats, in plain words:

- Trust base. The referee relies on FLINT root isolation, Arb ball arithmetic and its integrator, the python-flint bindings and the correctness of its own integrand logic, which its analytic controls exercise. It is an AI referee, not a human or a proof assistant. It does not know the producer's model; the task spec says it differs from its own.
- P1, P2 and P4 vanish on the circle. P1 = X, P2 = X-1 and P4 (through its order-10 cyclotomic factor) have zeros of their compositions on |z|=1. The referee found that P4 does too, which its input PROOF.md and attack note had not mentioned (the producer's REPORT.md names only P1 and P2). These zeros only add minus-infinity points to g and are harmless; its checker handles them with one-sided bounds rather than excision.
- The B enclosure. The referee's enclosure at integrator tolerance 2^-60 is wider than mine (about 2.1e-13) because Arb's absolute tolerance applies per subinterval, but is far inside the margin to 0.2519694759. A coarser run at tolerance 2^-50 gave a wider enclosure whose upper end is above the bound; that run is too coarse to decide and refutes nothing, and the claimed value lies inside it. A 2^-70 run was stopped unfinished and is not used.
- References. PROOF.md cites files the referee was not given (RESULT.json, verify.py, NUMERICS.md and an earlier referee's extraction argument); none was needed, and it recomputed D, a and the 228 pairs itself.
